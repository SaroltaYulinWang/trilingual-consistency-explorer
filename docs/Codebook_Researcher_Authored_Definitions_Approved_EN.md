# Codebook v0.2 — Researcher-Authored Definitions (Approved English)

## 1. Core Meaning Preserved

Normal differences in grammar, wording, or common expressions between Chinese, English, and Hungarian do not count as a meaning change. As long as both translations carry the same main message and purpose, these natural differences are acceptable.

## 2. Acceptable Variation

A noticeable, non-routine change in word choice, tone, or viewpoint can be acceptable if the main message and communicative purpose are preserved. The change just needs to make good sense for the context or fit how people naturally speak in that language.

## 3. Divergent Interpretations

Sometimes the English pivot sentence has more than one valid meaning. When this happens, the Chinese and Hungarian translations might pick different meanings, and both choices are reasonable.

## 4. Potential Accuracy Issue

You might suspect an issue when a specific part of a translation seems to add, drop, or change a meaning supported by the English pivot without a good reason. However, this is just a warning sign to double-check, not proof of a definite mistake.

## 5. Indeterminate

You cannot make a reliable classification when important context or other evidence needed to interpret the sentences is missing. This label simply means there is not enough information to judge, not that the translations are wrong.
